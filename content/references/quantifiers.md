# Quantifiers, comparison and nouns made from adjectives

A **Quantor / quantifier** expresses quantity or scope. Start by deciding whether you mean every member, an unspecified number, or a limited part of a group. These are different claims: **Alle Anträge sind geprüft** includes every application; **einige Anträge sind geprüft** leaves others open. **Nicht alle Anträge** means not all, and does not mean none.

| Meaning               | Nominative plural   | Dative plural              | What to notice                                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| All members           | alle neuen Anträge  | mit allen neuen Anträgen   | After inflected alle, the adjective normally has weak -en.                 |
| Both members          | beide neuen Geräte  | mit beiden neuen Geräten   | Beide refers to two; the adjective uses -en here.                          |
| An unspecified number | einige neue Anträge | mit einigen neuen Anträgen | In these examples, adjective and quantifier carry parallel strong endings. |
| A large number        | viele neue Anträge  | mit vielen neuen Anträgen  | Do not copy _alle neuen_ mechanically after viele.                         |
| No members            | keine neuen Anträge | mit keinen neuen Anträgen  | Kein follows the ein-word pattern, including plural forms.                 |

These examples describe core standard patterns. Words such as **manch, sämtlich, solch** have construction-dependent variants; look at the complete phrase rather than treating every quantity word as a definite article. **Jeder neue Antrag** is singular even when it describes a whole class.

An adjective can become the head of a noun phrase. Capitalize it and keep its adjective endings: **der Angestellte, ein Angestellter, die Angestellte, eine Angestellte, die Angestellten, mit Angestellten**. Gender can refer to the person; grammatical neuter **das Neue** refers to something abstract. Compare **Wir besprechen etwas Neues** with **Wir helfen dem neuen Kollegen**: the first has a nominalized adjective, the second an adjective plus a noun.

Comparison separates similarity and degree: **so zuverlässig wie**, **zuverlässiger als**, **am zuverlässigsten**. In an attributed phrase the comparative still declines: **mit einem zuverlässigeren Verfahren**. Avoid _mehr besser_. Some common forms are irregular: **gut – besser – am besten; viel – mehr – am meisten**. A claim such as _das beste Verfahren_ requires a comparison criterion; make that criterion explicit in professional writing.

Worked repair: **mit alle neue Mitarbeiter** → **mit allen neuen Mitarbeitern**. First identify dative after _mit_. Then form **allen**, the weak adjective **neuen**, and dative plural **Mitarbeitern**. These endings follow one case decision; do not diagnose three unrelated misunderstandings.

Try first: Explain the difference between **Nicht alle neuen Geräte sind freigegeben** and **Keine neuen Geräte sind freigegeben**. Then write a new comparison using _als_ and one using _wie_.

Feedback: The first permits some approved devices; the second rules out approval for the entire specified group. A correct comparison identifies both items and uses _als_ after a comparative, _wie_ in an equality comparison.
