## 5. Build a complete article and noun system

Create a permanent **der–die–das toolkit**, beginning with the basics and remaining available throughout the course.

Teach grammatical gender separately from case: a noun's stored gender does not change just because its article changes in a sentence. Distinguish singular feminine **die** from plural **die**, and explain grammatical gender versus a person's gender.

Include:

- Complete definite-article tables for four cases and all genders/plural; indefinite articles, possessives, **kein**, and zero articles; contractions such as **im**, **am**, and **zum**.
- Reliable derivational patterns, useful tendencies, major exceptions, and words that require individual learning. Explain that a true suffix is different from coincidentally matching final letters.
- Feminine suffix patterns such as **-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -tion, -ität**; neuter diminutives **-chen/-lein** and nominalized infinitives; appropriate masculine patterns such as **-ismus**. Check the scope of every rule and label tendencies honestly.
- Compound nouns and their final noun/head; word families; meaning differences associated with different genders; common professional nouns; and plural formation.
- Visual groupings, memorable mini-stories, sound associations, contrast pairs, and personal mnemonics. Explain that mnemonics support learning; they do not make every article predictable.
- Practice from identifying gender to using the noun correctly with case and adjective endings in spontaneous sentences.

Teach nouns as packages: **article + noun + plural + meaning + useful phrase + example**. Mark nouns without a usual plural. Separate gender errors, case errors, noun-ending errors, and adjective-ending errors in my error log.

Include a quick decision guide for an unfamiliar noun and a personal article deck built from my mistakes. Do not teach shortcuts such as “all words ending in -e are feminine” as universal rules.

## 6. Comprehensive grammar, organized for use

Build an indexed grammar reference and a spiral curriculum that revisits structures in increasingly demanding communication. Cover at least:

| Area | Required coverage |
|---|---|
| Noun phrases | Gender, plural, four cases, articles, strong/weak/mixed adjective declension, n-declension, genitive, nominalized adjectives, pronouns, determiners, quantifiers, comparison |
| Verbs | Regular/irregular forms, separable/inseparable verbs, reflexives, valency, common tense contrasts, modals, future and assumptions, imperative |
| Mood and voice | Konjunktiv II, Konjunktiv I and reported speech, unreal conditions, wishes, polite requests, passive forms and alternatives, modal meanings and speaker attitude |
| Sentence construction | Verb-second order, inversion, sentence brackets, verb-final clauses, middle-field choices, information structure, negation and scope, questions, relevant complex verb clusters |
| Relationships between clauses | Relative clauses, infinitive clauses, causal/consecutive/concessive/conditional/final/temporal/modal clauses, paired connectors, punctuation |
| Prepositions | Case government, two-way prepositions, verb/adjective/noun combinations, da-/wo-compounds; distinguish location and destination rather than using “movement” as a blanket rule |
| Advanced expression | Nominalization and verbalization, participial attributes, expanded noun phrases, functional verb constructions, word formation, precision, hedging, emphasis |
| Discourse and correctness | Cohesion, reference chains, topic development, register, idiomatic formulation, capitalization, spelling, commas, and paragraph structure |

For each topic, include: its communicative purpose; plain explanation; formation and usage; a visual or table where helpful; graded examples; English comparison where useful; common errors; exceptions; controlled practice; a workplace application; retrieval questions; explained solutions; and spaced reviews.

Treat word-order mnemonics as aids with limits, not absolute laws. Teach when a simpler sentence is clearer than a nominalized one. Do not equate C1 with long sentences or bureaucratic language.

Develop each skill explicitly:

- **Reading:** gist, detail, inference, attitude, implicit meaning, argument structure, and dense professional texts.
