# Pronunciation: listen, notice, record, transfer

Spelling is a clue, not a complete pronunciation guide. Keep a word's stress with its lexical entry, listen to a checked model when available and record an original sentence. Sound-dependent feedback requires the actual recording. A transcript can hide both pronunciation and grammar errors.

| Target          | What to notice                                                     | Practice                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vowel length    | Vowel quality often changes as well as duration.                   | bieten / bitten; distinguish in a complete sentence.       |
| Umlauts         | Lip shape and tongue position both matter.                         | schon / schön; Bruder / Brüder.                            |
| ich / ach       | Front versus back fricative, shaped by surrounding sounds.         | ich / auch; nicht every ch has the same sound.             |
| Final devoicing | Word-final written b/d/g may be pronounced voiceless.              | Tag / Tage; compare the ending with the extended form.     |
| Stress          | Compounds often emphasize the first part; prefixes and loans vary. | ANrufen / verSTEHen; learn lexical exceptions.             |
| Sentence focus  | Stress highlights the information being contrasted.                | **ICH** schicke den Bericht / ich schicke **DEN BERICHT**. |
| Chunking        | Pauses group meaning rather than break every word.                 | Nach der Prüfung / erhalten Sie / eine Rückmeldung.        |

Do not turn these tendencies into universal spelling rules. For example, ch in borrowed words and regional varieties needs lexical context. Standard intelligibility is the target; erasing every accent is unnecessary.

Shadowing: hear a short clip, mark chunks and stress, speak with it, then record the meaning in a new sentence without reading. The final transfer is different evidence from imitation. Reduce speed during study when useful; the original speed remains relevant for later practice. A synthetic voice must be labeled and reviewed before it becomes a reference model.
