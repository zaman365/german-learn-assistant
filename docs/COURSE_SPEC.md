## 4. Diagnose and build the B2–C1 bridge

Start with a manageable diagnostic in short rounds, covering articles, cases, adjective endings, word order, verb forms, vocabulary, reading, writing, and—when the environment supports audio—listening and speaking. Include both recognition and independent production. Separate unassessed skills from weak skills.

Use D1 grammar/production, D2 reading, D3 writing, D4 recorded speaking/follow-ups, and D5 real listening. Split rounds into manageable segments and start useful teaching before all rounds finish. The map gives actual task types and provisional time budgets. If a modality or scorer is unavailable, preserve the work and show precisely what remains unassessed.

Give each bridge module an evidence-linked route: **skip** redundant teaching, **check** with a short transfer task, **repair** a specific weakness, or teach the **full** module. These are placement decisions, not mastery states. A diagnostic waiver can let me advance without falsely marking skipped lessons completed or a skill retained. Reassess when later performance contradicts placement.

The bridge must cover:

1. Sentence structure, finite verbs, sentence brackets, and main versus subordinate clauses.
2. Gender, articles, plural, cases, adjective endings, pronouns, and weak nouns.
3. Common verb patterns, tenses, modals, reflexive verbs, separable verbs, passive, and Konjunktiv II.
4. Prepositions, case government, connectors, relative clauses, and infinitive constructions.
5. Essential workplace vocabulary, collocations, email conventions, and spoken interaction.
6. Summarizing, paraphrasing, comparing, explaining causes, making proposals, and supporting opinions.
7. Listening for gist and detail, reading efficiently, pronunciation, and repairing misunderstandings.
8. Side-by-side B2 and C1 responses showing exactly what improves: precision, cohesion, nuance, register, and flexibility.

Provide brief prerequisite lessons below B2 whenever the diagnostic reveals a gap. Make this normal and targeted, without restarting everything I already know. Use a bridge checkpoint and specific follow-up tasks to decide what is ready for Part I.

## 5. Build a complete article and noun system

Create a permanent **der–die–das toolkit**, beginning with the basics and remaining available throughout the course.

Teach grammatical gender separately from case: a noun's stored gender does not change just because its article changes in a sentence. Distinguish singular feminine **die** from plural **die**, and explain grammatical gender versus a person's gender.

Include:

- Complete definite-article tables for four cases and all genders/plural; indefinite articles, possessives, **kein**, and zero articles; contractions such as **im**, **am**, and **zum**.
- Reliable derivational patterns, useful tendencies, major exceptions, and words that require individual learning. Explain that a true suffix is different from coincidentally matching final letters.
- Feminine suffix patterns such as **-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -tion, -ität**; neuter diminutives **-chen/-lein** and nominalized infinitives; appropriate masculine patterns such as **-ismus**. Check the scope of every rule and label tendencies honestly.
- Compound nouns and their final noun/head; word families; meaning differences associated with different genders; common professional nouns; and plural formation.
- Visual groupings, memorable mini-stories, sound associations, contrast pairs, and personal mnemonics. Explain that mnemonics support learning; they do not make every article predictable.
- Practice from identifying gender to using the noun correctly with case and adjective endings in spontaneous sentences.

Teach nouns as packages: **article + noun + plural + meaning + useful phrase + example**. Mark nouns without a usual plural. Separate gender errors, case errors, noun-ending errors, and adjective-ending errors in my error log.

Include a quick decision guide for an unfamiliar noun and a personal article deck built from my mistakes. Do not teach shortcuts such as “all words ending in -e are feminine” as universal rules.

Start from [GRAMMAR_REFERENCE.md](GRAMMAR_REFERENCE.md), which supplies real tables, examples, a decision diagram, and correction checks. Index nouns by lexical sense so forms such as **der Leiter** and **die Leiter** are not treated as one contradictory entry. Teach dative plural exceptions, weak and mixed genitives, and preposition-specific temporal case explicitly: **mit den Teams**, **des Kunden**, **des Namens**, **über das Wochenende**. Explain foreign plural stem changes rather than teaching “just add -en.” Expand this starter into the full searchable atlas required below.

## 6. Comprehensive grammar, organized for use

Build an indexed grammar reference and a spiral curriculum that revisits structures in increasingly demanding communication. Cover at least:

| Area | Required coverage |
|---|---|
| Noun phrases | Gender, plural, four cases, articles, strong/weak/mixed adjective declension, n-declension, genitive, nominalized adjectives, pronouns, determiners, quantifiers, comparison |
| Verbs | Regular/irregular forms, separable/inseparable verbs, reflexives, valency, common tense contrasts, modals, future and assumptions, imperative |
| Mood and voice | Konjunktiv II, Konjunktiv I and reported speech, unreal conditions, wishes, polite requests, passive forms and alternatives, modal meanings and speaker attitude |
| Sentence construction | Verb-second order, inversion, sentence brackets, verb-final clauses, middle-field choices, information structure, negation and scope, questions, relevant complex verb clusters |
| Relationships between clauses | Relative clauses, infinitive clauses, causal/consecutive/concessive/conditional/final/temporal/modal clauses, paired connectors, punctuation |
| Prepositions | Case government, two-way prepositions, verb/adjective/noun combinations, da-/wo-compounds; distinguish location and destination rather than using “movement” as a blanket rule |
| Advanced expression | Nominalization and verbalization, participial attributes, expanded noun phrases, functional verb constructions, word formation, precision, hedging, emphasis |
| Discourse and correctness | Cohesion, reference chains, topic development, register, idiomatic formulation, capitalization, spelling, commas, and paragraph structure |

For each topic, include: its communicative purpose; plain explanation; formation and usage; a visual or table where helpful; graded examples; English comparison where useful; common errors; exceptions; controlled practice; a workplace application; retrieval questions; explained solutions; and spaced reviews.

Treat word-order mnemonics as aids with limits, not absolute laws. Teach when a simpler sentence is clearer than a nominalized one. Do not equate C1 with long sentences or bureaucratic language.

## 7. Vocabulary, pronunciation, and all communication skills

Create thematic and searchable vocabulary banks covering applications and interviews, workplace organization, projects, customer service, complaints, sales, purchasing, logistics, quality, conflict, leadership, presentations, change, digitalization, sustainability, training, and cross-sector workplace topics.

Use complete lexical entries:

- Nouns: article, plural, meaning, pronunciation/stress, collocations, and example.
- Verbs: principal forms where needed, auxiliary, separability/reflexivity, required case or preposition, collocations, and example.
- Adjectives and expressions: usage, register, dependent prepositions where relevant, natural partners, contrasts, and example.

Include synonyms with meaningful differences, antonyms, word families, confusing pairs, false friends, idioms, and reusable sentence frames. Distinguish receptive from productive vocabulary. Start with a manageable new-item load, provisionally 8–12 useful items/chunks per study day, and adjust to retention and review burden. Do not present a word-count target as proof of C1.

Develop each skill explicitly:

- **Reading:** gist, detail, inference, attitude, implicit meaning, argument structure, and dense professional texts.
- **Listening:** natural-speed conversations, calls, meetings, announcements, competing viewpoints, numbers, dates, and note-taking.
- **Speaking:** explaining processes, presentations, interviews, small talk, turn-taking, clarification, disagreement, negotiation, collaborative problem-solving, and spontaneous follow-up questions.
- **Writing:** clear emails, complaints and replies, proposals, summaries, minutes, reports, reasoned positions, editing, and appropriate tone.
- **Mediation:** convey relevant information from a text or conversation to a different audience while preserving meaning, uncertainty, and speaker attribution; never add unsupported details.
- **Pronunciation:** vowel length, umlauts, relevant consonant contrasts, word and sentence stress, rhythm, intonation, intelligibility, and shadowing.

Use cycles of input → noticing → supported practice → independent output → feedback → later transfer. Provide optional advanced language without obscuring the essential material. Real-life practice is encouraged, but no required lesson should depend on finding an external partner or text.
