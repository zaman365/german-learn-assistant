# Reading and listening strategies

**Gist:** What is the purpose, topic and speaker's position? **Detail:** Which fact answers this question? **Inference:** What follows from the evidence although it is not said directly? Treat these as separate checks.

Use the question to decide where to listen or read closely. Matching tasks require comparing several plausible options; repeated vocabulary alone is not a match. A distractor may mention a real topic but reverse a date, condition or speaker's attitude. In an inference task, prefer the smallest conclusion the evidence supports.

For listening, preview the questions and predict categories (name, date, cause, action), not the answers. Take short notes with explicit labels. On replay in study mode, first locate the missed segment; then use the transcript to connect sound and meaning. A transcript is support, and work after seeing it must be marked assisted. Simulation follows its configured playback rules.

Common traps: a speaker corrects themselves (**am Dienstag — Entschuldigung, am Donnerstag**); a proposal is rejected later; a past process is contrasted with the current process; a negative restriction is spoken quickly. Listen to the whole relevant turn.

Audio that has not loaded cannot produce listening evidence. Reading the same content assesses a different modality. A missing or failed recording is **unassessed**, not a score of zero.
