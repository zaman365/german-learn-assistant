# Negation, focus and information order

**Satzklammer / sentence bracket** is the frame made by the finite verb and a final verb part: **Wir haben den Auftrag noch nicht bestätigt**. The middle field contains the material between them. It has tendencies, not one compulsory order for every sentence.

**Kein** usually negates an indefinite or articleless noun phrase: **Wir haben keinen Vertrag**. **Nicht** can deny a whole proposition or one focused part. Ask what is being contradicted before deciding its position.

| Intended correction               | German                                                  | What stays possible                  |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| A different time                  | Wir liefern nicht heute, sondern morgen.                | Delivery still happens.              |
| A different recipient             | Wir informieren nicht den Kunden, sondern die Kollegin. | Someone is informed.                 |
| A different action                | Wir bestätigen den Auftrag nicht.                       | The order may still be under review. |
| No item exists for us             | Wir haben keinen bestätigten Auftrag.                   | There may be an unconfirmed inquiry. |
| An action has not happened so far | Wir haben noch nicht bestellt.                          | Ordering later remains possible.     |
| An action has ceased              | Wir bestellen nicht mehr bei diesem Betrieb.            | Earlier orders are implied.          |

Do not replace **noch nicht** with **nicht mehr** in a customer update. That changes the time relationship. Likewise **nur** restricts what follows in focus: **Nur Frau Kaya prüft die Rechnung** restricts the person; **Frau Kaya prüft nur die Rechnung** restricts what she checks. Spoken stress may refine interpretation, so ask for clarification when a written sentence remains ambiguous.

A common neutral order puts known information before new information and unstressed pronouns before full noun phrases: **Ich schicke ihm die Unterlagen**; with two unstressed object pronouns, **Ich schicke sie ihm**. With two full noun phrases, **Ich schicke dem Kunden die Unterlagen** is a useful starting pattern. Focus can change order: **Die Unterlagen schicke ich dem Kunden morgen** makes the documents the topic. The finite verb remains in second position, even when the first constituent has several words.

Worked revision: **Morgen ich kann nicht den Kunden anrufen** → **Morgen kann ich den Kunden nicht anrufen** if the call cannot happen. If a different person will be called, use **Morgen kann ich nicht den Kunden, sondern die Kollegin anrufen**. Word order and scope should preserve the intended message.

Independent check: A colleague believes you rejected the whole order. Explain instead that only the Friday delivery date is unconfirmed, and the goods themselves are available. Feedback should check that your negation targets the date and that you do not promise another unapproved date.
